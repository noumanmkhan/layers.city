When Toronto calls an Uber

Toronto took 87.1 million ride-hail trips in 2025, a record. They don't come evenly. On an average Monday to Thursday, about 246,000 rides begin each day; on a Friday, 316,000. Move the clock and switch the day to see where and when.

    Is there a car?

    The busiest hour for riders isn't the hardest one for drivers. At 8 a.m. on a weekday, drivers spend only 14% of their logged-in time waiting for a request: the city's tightest hour. In the small hours, they wait about 47% of the time.

    Drivers logged in across the city in each hour, from a sample of four months (October, January, April and July). The City publishes driver time city-wide, not by place, so this can't say where idle drivers are. A driver working two apps is counted once.

    Where people go

    Downtown's three wards trade riders all day. At 1 a.m. on a Saturday night, the busiest trip from one ward to another is Spadina-Fort York to Toronto Centre.

    Since 2018

    Pickups fell by more than half in 2020 and 2021. They passed 2019's level in 2024 and set a record in 2025.

    How this was counted

    The City of Toronto requires ride-hail companies (Uber, Lyft and others licensed as private transportation companies) to report every trip and every stretch of time a driver is available. Since March 2026 the City has published those records as open data, combined across companies and summed by hour and ward.

    This page uses every completed trip from August 1, 2025 to July 30, 2026: 91.5 million of them, including trips that start in the GTA outside Toronto and at Pearson. A day here runs from 4 a.m. to 4 a.m., so a 1 a.m. pickup on Saturday counts as Friday night. Holidays are left out, and each hour shows the average over all days of that kind (198 Mon–Thu, 50 Friday, 52 Saturday, 52 Sunday).

    Pickups are counted in the ward where the trip began and the hour it began. Drivers are counted city-wide: a driver is logged in during an hour if any of it was spent available, heading to a pickup, waiting or on a trip.

    What it leaves out: rides that were requested but never completed (the City's cancellation counts changed in January 2026), anything about price surges (the City's data has none), food delivery, taxis and limousines.
