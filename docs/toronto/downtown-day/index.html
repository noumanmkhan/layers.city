Toronto · Downtown, hour by hour

Every weekday, downtown Toronto nearly doubles

About 313,000 people live downtown. By 1 p.m. on an average weekday, 583,000 people are there: residents plus everyone who came in by subway, GO train, car, bike or on foot. Then it drains. Move the clock to watch it happen.

How they got here

At 1 p.m., almost half of the people who came in rode the TTC. Count every trip in over the whole day, though, and cars bring the most. Drivers come and go; GO riders stay all day.

Where downtown's workers live

677,000 people have their usual workplace downtown. About one in five lives there too. Two in three live somewhere in Toronto, and one in three commutes from outside the city, some from as far as Owen Sound.

      And the other half?

      Of downtown's 677,000 workers, 531,000 live outside downtown. Yet at the busiest hour, only about 270,000 more people are downtown than at 4 a.m., and that count includes students, shoppers and anyone else who came in. On an average weekday, at least half of downtown's commuters aren't there.

      The survey's weekdays lean toward Thursdays and Fridays, when fewer people go in, so a typical Tuesday or Wednesday is likely busier.

      How this was counted

      The numbers come from the 2022 Transportation Tomorrow Survey, a travel survey of about 5% of households across the Greater Golden Horseshoe, taken in fall 2022 and spring 2023 and weighted to the whole population. Each household reported every trip its members aged 5 and up made on one weekday.

      Downtown here is the survey's planning district 1: roughly Bathurst Street to the Don River, from the lake to about Bloor Street and the Rosedale ravine, plus Liberty Village, the Exhibition grounds, the Distillery District, the Port Lands and the Toronto Islands.

      We start at 4 a.m. with the district's 313,000 residents, then add every trip that ended downtown and subtract every trip that left it, hour by hour, by the hour each trip started. Trips that began and ended downtown don't change the count.

      What it leaves out: tourists, business travellers and anyone who lives outside the survey area, and children under 5 on their own. So evenings, when visitors matter most, run low. Times are when trips began, so arrivals from far away land a little later than shown. Weekends aren't surveyed.
