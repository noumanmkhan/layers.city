Privacy

How the Layers.City iPhone app and the maps on this site handle your information. The short version: no accounts, no ads, no tracking cookies, and nothing about you is sold. The website counts visits and which features get used, without identifying anyone.

What changed on October 21, 2026: the iPhone app was rebuilt as Layers.City, with optional background location, iCloud sync for your saved home and the neighbourhoods you’ve been to, on-phone reminders, and address search with Apple Maps. Its section is rewritten; the website’s is unchanged.

In short

  • There are no accounts, no sign-ups, no advertising and no tracking cookies. The app uses no analytics.
  • The website counts visits and which features get used (for example “someone picked the Getting around view”), with Umami. It doesn’t identify you, and you can opt out. Cloudflare, the site’s host, also measures page loads and speed, without cookies (details).
  • Your location is optional, is only used when you ask for it, and stays on your device. The app updates in the background only if you turn that on. Pins, searches and place names are never counted.
  • If you use iCloud, the app’s saved home and the neighbourhoods you’ve been to sync through your own iCloud account, which I can’t see.
  • Searching for an address sends what you type to a search service (Apple in the app, OpenStreetMap on the website) so it can find the place.
  • Your settings are saved on your own device, not on a server run by me.
  • I don’t sell, rent or share personal information, because I don’t collect it.

Layers.City app (iPhone)

What the app is

Layers.City for iPhone shows where you are in a city (neighbourhood, ward, representatives), what’s going on around you (pickup days, road and transit closures, where to vote) and what’s on this weekend, for the cities on this site. It is made by Nouman Khan, an individual developer. It has no accounts and no server of its own: everything it shows comes from the same public files as the maps on this site.

Your location

Location access is optional, and the app works without it: it shows a sample spot, or a spot you choose. There are two levels, each asked for only when you turn on the feature that needs it:

  • While using the app (“Use my location”): to show what’s around where you are.
  • Always (“Update in the background”, off unless you turn it on): so the widget follows you, and so the app can note the neighbourhoods you spend time in. It uses iOS’s low-power “significant change” and “visit” updates, not continuous tracking.

Working out which neighbourhood, ward or district a spot is in happens on your phone. Your location is never sent to me or to any server of mine, never stored on a server, never sold, and never used for advertising. You can change or turn off location access at any time in iOS Settings.

Neighbourhoods you’ve been to

With “Update in the background” on, the app records the neighbourhoods you’ve stopped in (passing through doesn’t count), or ones you mark yourself with “I’ve been here”. It keeps only the neighbourhood’s name and when you were first and last there: never coordinates or routes. A spot you choose in Settings never counts as a visit.

Saved places and iCloud

Your saved home (its name and position) and the neighbourhoods you’ve been to are stored on your phone and, if you use iCloud, synced through your own iCloud account (Apple’s CloudKit private database) so they follow you to a new phone. Apple stores them under your Apple Account; I can’t see them. Without iCloud they stay on your phone. Delete my history in Settings removes them from your phone and from iCloud.

Searching for a spot

When you choose a spot in Settings, neighbourhood and place names are matched on your phone as you type, and nothing is sent. Addresses and postal codes are looked up with Apple Maps, and only when you press Search: what you type goes to Apple to find the place, under Apple’s privacy policy. Layers.City keeps no record of it.

Reminders

Pickup reminders, the Friday weekend summary and other notifications are optional and asked for only when you turn them on. They’re scheduled on your phone from the published data; there is no notification server that knows who or where you are.

Data the app downloads

The app comes with a copy of each city’s data so it works offline, and downloads updates from layers.city: the list of cities, each city’s map data and the weekend summary. Those downloads are ordinary file requests: they don’t include your location, searches or any identifier, though, as with any web request, the server that delivers the files sees your device’s IP address (see the website section). The data is public open data, credited in the app’s Settings and on the Attributions page.

Pages and maps it opens

Fact sheets, venue pages and the full map open from layers.city in a browser sheet inside the app; those are this website, so the website section applies, including its visit counts. Tapping a count such as “6 bike share stations” opens Apple Maps searching near that spot, under Apple’s privacy policy.

What’s stored on your phone

Your settings (such as reminder time and a chosen spot) and a small summary of where you are, which the widgets read, are kept in storage shared only between the app and its own widgets. Deleting the app deletes them.

What the app doesn’t do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No analytics or tracking tools, and no advertising.
  • No collection of contacts, photos, health data or other personal information.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data with anyone.

If you’ve allowed iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send me anonymous crash reports; you control that in iOS Settings, under Privacy & Security.

This website

This covers layers.city: the maps page and every city’s map.

No accounts, ads or tracking cookies

You don’t need an account, there is no advertising, and the site sets no tracking cookies.

Live bus, streetcar and subway times

On the Events page, “Near me” sorts the listing by distance from you in your browser. Your location isn’t sent anywhere, saved, put in the page’s address or counted; it’s forgotten when you leave the page.

On the Where does this line or route go? page, “Find stops near me” works out the stops around you in your browser; your location isn’t sent anywhere. To show when the next buses, streetcars and trains come, the page then asks layers.city/api/arrivals for the times at those stops. That request carries only the stop numbers. The site reads the TTC’s public live feeds to answer it, keeps nothing about the request, and sets no cookies; as with any web request, Cloudflare, which hosts the site, sees your IP address.

Counting visits and feature use

To learn which cities and features people find useful, the site uses Umami Cloud, a privacy-focused analytics service that sets no cookies (Umami’s privacy policy). It receives:

  • the page you’re on, as its plain address (for example layers.city/toronto/), and the website that linked you here;
  • your browser, device type, screen size and language, and your country, region and city as Umami estimates them from your connection;
  • the names of features you use, such as which View you picked, which layer you turned on, that you started the tour or saved an image in the portrait shape.

It never receives your location, the pins you drop, what you search for, the names of places you look at, or anything after the ? or # in the address (where pins and place names live in shared links). The site checks every message before it goes out and drops anything that isn’t a plain feature name. Umami doesn’t identify you, and the counts are only ever seen as totals.

Nothing is counted if your browser sends Global Privacy Control, or if you opt out here. The site doesn’t act on the older Do Not Track setting: the counts hold no personal details and use no cookies, so they’re totals rather than tracking.

The opt-out is saved in this browser only; clearing your site data undoes it.

Page loads and speed (Cloudflare)

Cloudflare, which hosts the site, adds a small script of its own (Cloudflare Web Analytics) that reports each page load: the page’s address (Cloudflare says it doesn’t record the part after the ?), the website that linked you here, your browser and device type, your country as Cloudflare estimates it, and how quickly the page loaded. It sets no cookies and doesn’t identify you (how Cloudflare Web Analytics works). It isn’t told your location or which features you use. I see only totals, such as page views a day. The opt-out button above covers Umami only; blocking scripts from cloudflareinsights.com in your browser stops this one.

Hosting and the services the page loads

The site is hosted on Cloudflare Pages. Like any website, loading it means your browser connects to some outside services, which receive your IP address and basic browser details as part of that connection:

  • Cloudflare, which hosts the pages and map data (Cloudflare’s privacy policy).
  • Google Fonts, for the map’s typeface (Google’s privacy policy).
  • cdnjs (Cloudflare) and jsDelivr, which serve the open-source map libraries.
  • Umami, for the visit counts described above.
  • Cloudflare Web Analytics, for the page-load counts described above.

I don’t receive or keep these services’ logs. From Cloudflare I see only traffic totals, such as the number of requests a day, and from Umami only totals such as visits by city or how often a feature is used.

Address search

When you search for an address, what you type is sent to OpenStreetMap’s Nominatim service to find it (OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy). Suggestions for landmarks and place names come from the map’s own data, in your browser, and aren’t sent anywhere.

Your location and pins

On phones, the map has a “Use my location” button. The website only asks for your location when you tap it, and it never leaves your device: working out what’s there happens in your browser, it isn’t counted, and it isn’t added to links you share. When you drop a pin, working out what it falls in also happens in your browser. Shareable links keep the map position and pin in the part of the link after the #, which browsers don’t send to the server; it only goes to whoever you share the link with.

Reporting a problem

“Report a problem” at the foot of the “What’s here” card opens your own email app with a message to go@layers.city ready to edit. The page sends nothing itself: the email goes only if you press Send, and like any email it shows me your email address. The place you’re looking at is added only if you tick “Include this place”, which starts unticked. The visit counter records only that the button was used, not what you wrote or where.

Saving images

The “Image” button creates the picture in your browser. The first time you use it, a small open-source library is loaded from jsDelivr.

What’s stored in your browser

The site saves a few display settings in your browser’s local storage: light or dark theme, whether the side panel is folded, which card sections are open, whether you’ve seen the tour, and your visit-counting opt-out if you choose one. These are not cookies, aren’t sent anywhere, and you can clear them by clearing your browser’s site data.
