How it’s built

The idea, the principles and the architecture behind Layers.City: open city data turned into one map that keeps itself current, and how I built it with AI as an engineering partner.

Read the write-up

Not a snapshot

Most maps are frozen the day they’re published. These rebuild themselves from the cities’ own open data on a schedule: new bus schedules, building permits, road closures and election results show up on the map without anyone redrawing it.

Each live layer shows when it was last refreshed, and a daily check flags anything that falls behind. How that works.

DAILY
Road closures for transit construction (the Ontario Line, Eglinton West, GO Expansion, Chicago’s Red Line Extension), from city permits, just after midnight.
WEEKLY
Bus and streetcar routes, stops and how often they run, from TTC, GO, CTA, TransLink and DART schedules (Mondays). Who represents you: councillors, alderpersons, legislators, members of Congress and MPs (Mondays). What’s changing: new buildings, demolitions and development applications (Tuesdays).
MONTHLY
Skyscrapers, including towers still going up; transit lines under construction; heritage buildings; and libraries, community centres, parks and places of worship.
ELECTION NIGHT
Results every hour as polls report: Toronto’s school trustees and Chicago’s first elected Board of Education, then daily until the winners are official.
