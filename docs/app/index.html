Layers.City for iPhone

Know where you are and what’s going on around you, without opening anything. A lock screen widget, a few well-timed reminders, and a home screen of big numbers for the neighbourhood you’re in, in Toronto, Chicago, Vancouver, Dallas and New York.

What it does Testing Support

What it does

  • Where am I: a lock screen and home screen widget with your neighbourhood, your ward and one thing worth knowing today: a closure nearby, pickup tomorrow, or where to vote on election day.
  • Around you: who represents you, your next pickup, road closures and what’s changing nearby, and what the area has within a short walk. Tap a count, such as bike share stations, to see them in Apple Maps.
  • Pickup reminders: the evening before collection day at your saved home. In Chicago, a “move your car” reminder before street sweeping.
  • This weekend: what’s closed (subway shutdowns, event and major road closures) and what’s on at the big venues, with an optional Friday morning summary.
  • Been to: neighbourhood stamps for the places you’ve spent time in, if you turn that on.

The app is the glance; this website is the deep tool. Fact sheets, venue pages and the full maps open from layers.city inside the app. It describes places rather than ranking them: no “best” or “worst” neighbourhood scores. It has no accounts, ads or tracking; see the privacy policy.

Layers.City is an independent project by Nouman Khan. It isn’t affiliated with any city government or transit agency.

Adding the widget

Lock screen: touch and hold the lock screen, tap Customize, then Lock Screen, tap the area under the clock and choose Layers.City. Home screen: touch and hold an empty part of the home screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget, and choose Layers.City.

The widget shows the wrong place

Without background updates, the widget shows where you were the last time you opened the app. To have it follow you, turn on Update in the background in the app’s Settings and allow location Always when iOS asks. Or choose a spot in Settings to see a particular place.

Location is optional

The app works without it: it shows a sample spot, or one you choose by searching for a neighbourhood, address or postal code. To change location access, open iOS Settings, then Layers.City, then Location.

Reminders don’t arrive

Pickup reminders need a saved home in a city with collection data (Toronto, Chicago, Dallas and New York) and notifications allowed for Layers.City in iOS Settings. Opening the app now and then keeps the schedule fresh.

A boundary or fact looks wrong

The data comes from public sources: each city’s own open data, transit agencies, the national census (Statistics Canada and the U.S. Census Bureau) and OpenStreetMap, credited in the app’s Settings and on the Attributions page. Census figures are from the most recent release, so they lag a few years behind. If something looks wrong, email the spot and what you expected; local knowledge is exactly what’s useful.

Removing your data

Delete my history in the app’s Settings removes your saved home and the neighbourhoods you’ve been to, from your iPhone and from iCloud. Everything else the app keeps is on your iPhone and goes when you delete the app.
